Summary Takeaways
1. Pass-Through Elasticity: Bivariate OLS on monthly MoSPI CPI data (2014–2025, N = 142) yields an urban slope of β̂ = 1.0813 (p < 0.0001; Newey-West HAC SE = 0.0751, z = 14.40), accounting for 73.5% of variation in rural inflation.
2. Parity Testing: A joint Wald test fails to reject 45° parity [F(2, 140) = 2.046, p = 0.1331], confirming long-run spatial co-movement while capturing slight rural amplification (β > 1) during price increases.
3. Basket Composition: Food accounts for 54.18% of rural CPI compared to 36.29% in urban CPI (+17.89 pp), explaining higher rural volatility (σrural = 1.94% vs. σurban = 1.54%) and highlighting the role of supply-side agricultural policy alongside rate decisions.
